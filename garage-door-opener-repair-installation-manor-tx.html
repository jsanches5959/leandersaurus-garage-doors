Repair first, when repair is honest

  • Stripped drive gears — the classic failure on older chain-drive units: the motor runs, the door doesn’t
  • Safety sensors — misaligned, sun-blinded, or damaged wiring making the door refuse to close
  • Logic boards — cooked by Texas heat and storm surges; the opener acts possessed, or dies outright
  • Remotes, keypads, and wall controls — lost signal, worn buttons, plain reprogramming
  • Belts, chains, and trolleys — stretched, slack, or snapped drive parts

If your opener is a solid unit with one failed part, repair is the smart money. If it’s a twenty-year-old unit on its third repair, we’ll show you the repair price and the replacement price side by side — in writing — and let you pick.

Quiet is the upgrade

What a new opener gets a Manor home

Manor housing runs two ways. In the new subdivisions — ShadowGlen, Whisper Valley, Greenbury and the streets still rising off US-290 and FM 973 — the builder opener was chosen on price, not quiet or features, and it cycles hard from day one. Around the original townsite, older homes and detached garages can still run openers old enough to predate rolling-code security, where an old remote signal is far easier to copy. A modern belt-drive runs near-silent, adds LED lighting, phone control that tells you if the door got left open halfway down US-290, and battery backup.

That last one carries extra weight out here: storms rolling across open country east of Austin can leave Manor streets dark longer than in-town streets, and a battery-backed opener keeps the door working while the power company does its work. We’ll tell you which situation you actually have — security-era unit, tired builder unit, or a good opener with one failed part — before quoting either fix.

Straight answers

Questions, answered straight

How much does garage door opener repair cost in Manor, TX?

Typical Central Texas range: $150–$350 for most opener repairs — gears, sensors, boards, and controls. A diagnosis visit is free with us (most companies charge $65–$95), and your exact price is in writing before any work starts.

How much is a new garage door opener installed in Manor?

Typical Central Texas range: $450–$750 installed, depending on drive type and features like battery backup and phone control. The installed price is confirmed in writing before you commit — nothing gets priced off a phone guess.

Storms knock our power out east of Austin. Will a new opener still work?

With battery backup, yes — the door keeps running through an outage, which matters in Manor where open country east of Austin can wait longer for power to come back than denser in-town streets. Battery backup is one of the options we price in writing; if your current opener is otherwise healthy, we’ll say so instead of selling you a new one.

Can a new opener tell me if I left the door open?

Yes — the modern openers we install offer phone control: an alert if the door is left open, and the ability to check or close it from wherever you are. On a US-290 or SH-130 commute into Austin, that settles the “did I close it?” question without turning around. We’ll tell you which models in your written quote include it.

My remote only works when I’m right in front of the door. What’s going on?

Usually something simple: a dying remote battery, a loose antenna wire on the unit, or interference — some LED bulbs in or near the opener are notorious for swamping the signal. All are quick checks during a diagnosis visit. A failing receiver board is the less common, pricier cause, and we’ll show you which one you have before quoting anything.
